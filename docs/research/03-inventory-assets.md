## Executive summary

1. **The DEA EMS rule is new and in force.** DEA published the PPAEMA final rule on **Feb 5, 2026 (91 FR 5216, FR Doc. 2026-02288), effective Mar 9, 2026**. It codifies EMS agency registration (one per state where the agency operates), "designated locations" (unregistered stationhouses that must be reported to DEA at least 30 days before first delivery), storage rules (§1301.80), EMS recordkeeping (§1304.27), hospital restocking, and standing/verbal orders. This is a strong reason to build SomaOS's controlled-substance module now: vendors are actively marketing "PPAEMA compliance".
2. **NFPA consolidation is real, and the numbers have changed.** Product logic and labels should cite the new consolidated numbers, with the legacy numbers as aliases:
   - NFPA 1911 + 1912 + 1925 (+1071) → **NFPA 1910 (2024)**, covering in-service apparatus inspection, maintenance, testing, and retirement.
   - NFPA 1901 + 1906 + 1917 (ambulance) + 414 → **NFPA 1900 (2024)**, covering new apparatus and ambulance design.
   - NFPA 1971 + 1975 + 1981 + 1982 → **NFPA 1970 (2025)**, covering PPE, SCBA, and PASS design.
   - NFPA 1851 + 1852 → **NFPA 1850**, covering care and maintenance of turnout gear and SCBA. It is labeled the 2025 or 2026 edition (effective Sept 9, 2025) depending on the source.
   - NFPA 1802 + 1932 + 1937 + 1962 → **NFPA 1930 (2025)**, covering in-service ground ladders, hose, nozzles, appliances, rescue tools, radios, and thermal imagers.
   - NFPA 1931 + 1936 + 1961 + 1963 + 1964 → **NFPA 1960 (2024)**, covering design of ground ladders, hose, and appliances.
   - NFPA 291 (hydrant flow testing and marking) still stands on its own.
3. **The market is crowded but fragmented.** The main competitors are Operative IQ, PSTrax, ESO Logistics (Inventory, Asset Management, Checklists), First Due Assets & Inventory, and Vector Check It. Their common features: mobile checks, failed-check → work order, par/reorder, expirations, narcotics chain of custody. Only ESO clearly markets **ePCR → inventory depletion** (because it owns the ePCR). That integration is SomaOS's natural all-in-one advantage.
4. **Data-model implications:** a location tree of arbitrary depth, lot/expiry-level stock, an append-only ledger of signed transactions (dual signature for controlled drugs), a generic "asset + test/inspection schedule" engine driven by configurable standard templates, and asset status that flows into staffing and scheduling.

# PART A: Smart Inventory

## A1. Multi-location inventory hierarchy

**Industry pattern [VERIFIED-SECONDARY, vendor sources]:**
- Operative IQ manages "PAR and reorder point tracking" in supply rooms, learns usage to suggest reorder quantities, and tracks "real-field usage against individual calls". Its Front Line app captures quantity, expiration date, and lot number when managing distributed inventory. Sources: https://operativeiq.com/inventory-and-asset-management ; https://www.police1.com/police-products/police-technology/software/quartermaster-software/company-spotlight-operative-iq-helps-eliminate-the-guesswork-in-public-safety-logistics ; https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1616940091
- First Due supports "custom compartments to track equipment on each vehicle" and "kit management". Source: https://firstdue.com/products/assetsinventory
- In ESO Inventory, crews record rig stock checks and restock vehicles. Source: https://www.eso.com/inventory/ ; https://www.eso.com/wp-content/uploads/2023/04/ESO-1P-Inventory.pdf

**Recommended SomaOS model (design recommendation, not sourced):**
```
Location (self-referencing tree; type enum)
  ├─ type: WAREHOUSE | SUPPLY_ROOM | STATION | APPARATUS | COMPARTMENT | BAG/KIT | DRUG_BOX | SAFE | CABINET | MEMBER (for issued PPE)
  ├─ parent_location_id, sort_order, is_mobile (apparatus/bags move between stations)
  ├─ dea_role: NONE | REGISTERED_LOCATION | DESIGNATED_LOCATION   (controlled substances; see A3)
  └─ template_id (for "load-out templates": every ALS engine has the same compartments and pars)
StockLevel (location_id, item_id, lot_id?) → on_hand, par, min, max, reorder_point
```
- Kits and drug boxes should be **serializable containers**: a kit is both a location (it holds items) and an asset (it has a serial or seal number and moves between units). Seal-number tracking for sealed boxes belongs on the container.
- **Load-out templates** (an apparatus type → compartments → expected items and pars) let a department define "what belongs on Medic 7" once and diff actual stock against it. The template drives daily truck checks.

**ePCR-driven depletion:** ESO says that with ePCR integration, "usage of meds and other supplies is ... recorded via a pre-populated list in ESO Inventory as result of entering treatment into your ePCR" [VERIFIED-SECONDARY, vendor] (https://www.eso.com/wp-content/uploads/2021/05/ESO-Product-OneSheeter-Inventory_Final.pdf ; https://www.businesswire.com/news/home/20210715005342/en). No source found that Operative IQ has a direct ePCR link; its materials only say "supplies are automatically tracked as they're used". SomaOS design: post ePCR medication and procedure entries as *pending consumption* against the responding unit's location, have the crew confirm or adjust them, then generate a restock pick list from supply room → unit.

## A2. Lot/expiration, FEFO, recalls, barcodes, RFID

- **Lot and expiry capture in the field** is standard (Operative IQ Front Line, above). ESO markets "preventing overstocking and expiration of medication" [VERIFIED-SECONDARY, vendor].
- **FEFO** (first-expired-first-out) pick suggestions and expiry alert windows (for example 90/60/30 days) are table-stakes design recommendations **[UNVERIFIED as to specific competitor implementation]**.
- **GS1 / GTIN / FDA UDI / DataMatrix [UNVERIFIED, general knowledge, not researched due to search limit]:** Medical device labels carry a UDI under FDA rules (21 CFR Part 801 Subpart B; GUDID database). Prescription drug packages carry a product identifier, serial, lot, and expiry in a 2D DataMatrix under the DSCSA. GS1 Application Identifiers (01) GTIN, (17) expiry, (10) lot, and (21) serial let one scan fill item, lot, and expiry. Mobile scanning should parse GS1 AIs. **Verify before building.**
- **Recall matching [UNVERIFIED]:** openFDA exposes drug and device enforcement/recall endpoints that can be queried by product code, NDC, or lot. Matching recalls against stocked lots would be a differentiator. No competitor evidence was found either way.
- **RFID:** ESO Asset Management is described by a third-party listing as supporting "mobile barcode/RFID scanning" [VERIFIED-SECONDARY, third party] (https://intuitionlabs.ai/software/emergency-medical-services-ems/fleet-and-equipment-management/eso-asset-management). RFID drug-box and kit systems exist in the EMS market but were not researched **[UNVERIFIED]**.

### A3.3 Workflow requirements for SomaOS (derived)
- **Append-only controlled-substance ledger.** Every event is an immutable row: receive (from distributor via 222/CSOS), transfer registered → designated, transfer designated → designated, load to vehicle/box, administer (linked to the ePCR and the standing order), waste (witness required), return, destroy (Form 41), hospital restock (with the 72h notice timer to the registered location), theft/loss (Form 106 workflow and the one-business-day timer), count/inventory (shift change, biennial).
- **Dual signature.** Two authenticated users (separate credentials, optionally a PIN or biometric) on waste, shift-change counts, transfers, and seal breaks. Vector Check It advertises eSignatures and biometric verification for checks [VERIFIED-SECONDARY] (https://www.vectorsolutions.com/solutions/vector-check-it/).
- **Seal/box tracking.** A drug box is a container with a current seal number. Seal break → reason → new seal number → contents count.
- **Discrepancy workflow.** Count mismatch → hold → supervisor review → resolved (documented error) or escalated (theft/significant loss → Form 106 clock).
- **Per-state registration objects.** Registration(state, DEA number, expiry, schedules) → registered locations → designated locations (DEA notified date, eligible-for-delivery date = notified + 30 days).
- **Smart safes / vaults [UNVERIFIED].** Knox MedVault, NarcBox, and similar electronic-access vaults that keep an audit trail were not researched (search limit). No source confirmed that Operative IQ integrates with Knox. Plan a generic "access-event ingest" API (vault ID, user, timestamp, open/close) and reconcile it against ledger events.
- Competitor parity: Operative IQ has a Narcotics Tracking app for chain of custody and vial lifecycle (https://apps.apple.com/us/app/id1616940091). PSTrax tracks "every vial handoff ... arriving/departing checks, usage events, restocks, and inventory checks" (https://www.getapp.com/government-social-services-software/a/pstrax/).

## A4. Daily truck checks, O2, cots, shift narcotics counts
- **Checks are the core engagement loop** in every competitor. First Due offers drag-and-drop check forms, auto work orders on failed items, a Kanban work-order board, and a "checks due today" dashboard (https://firstdue.com/products/assetsinventory). Vector Check It offers offline native apps, timestamped auditable records, and failed check → service ticket (https://www.vectorsolutions.com/resources/blogs/fire-department-asset-management-software/). PSTrax emails specialists when inventory is low or equipment fails (https://www.firehouse.com/tech-comm/press-release/11311171/public-safety-tracking-system-pstrax-offers-new-way-of-automating-apparatus-equipment-checks).
- **Oxygen cylinders.** DOT 3AL aluminum cylinders (the common medical O2 cylinder) require requalification **every 5 years** at 5/3 of service pressure, with exceptions in 49 CFR 180.209(j) and (m). Older 6351-T6 alloy 3AL cylinders need eddy-current inspection (Part 180 Appendix C) because of sustained-load cracking. A 2026 PHMSA rule adds a 10-year requalification option in §180.209(b)(2); whether it applies to 3AL is **unconfirmed**. Sources: https://www.law.cornell.edu/cfr/text/49/180.209 ; https://www.ecfr.gov/current/title-49/subtitle-B/chapter-I/subchapter-C/part-180/subpart-C/section-180.209 ; https://www.govinfo.gov/content/pkg/FR-2006-08-29/html/E6-14255.htm ; https://public-inspection.federalregister.gov/2026-15819.pdf . **Model:** cylinder as a serialized asset (DOT spec, serial, manufacture date, last hydro date, next due, fill level/PSI per check, location).
- **Cots/stretchers and power-load systems [UNVERIFIED]:** usually on manufacturer preventive-maintenance schedules. Model them as an asset with a PM schedule.
- **Shift-change narcotics count:** see A3.3. Two-person count, with the variance captured.

## A6. Everyday station supplies and PPE issuance
- Station consumables (cleaning, kitchen, office, PPE consumables such as gloves and N95s) fit the same model as other items, with a "non-medical" category, no lot required, station-level pars, and a request → approve → fulfil workflow **[design recommendation]**.
- **PPE issuance to members:** item → size → issued to member (member as a location), issue/return/exchange, with a link to the serialized turnout gear asset (see B3). Vector Check It and PSTrax list PPE tracking [VERIFIED-SECONDARY, vendor]. Size profiles per member are an [UNVERIFIED] common practice.

## B2. Apparatus / fleet
- **Records:** VIN, unit ID, type (engine, ladder, medic, ...), NFPA 1900/1901 build year, mileage and engine/pump hours (captured at checks), status (in service / limited / OOS / reserve), assigned station, fuel logs **[fuel logs UNVERIFIED as competitor feature; standard fleet practice]**.
- **Out-of-service status must drive scheduling and staffing.** When a unit goes OOS, staffing and CAD need to know so crews can move to a reserve unit. This is a key all-in-one integration and a design recommendation. First Due links failed checks → work orders, and some products surface status on dashboards (sources above).
- **Pump testing:** annual fire pump service test, historically NFPA 1911 Chapter 21 (engine speed, pump shift indicator, vacuum, pumping at rated capacity/pressure, overload tests). It is now within NFPA 1910 (2024) under the consolidation; the chapter number changed and is not confirmed. Sources: https://www.ul.com/services/performance-testing-fire-department-pumps ; https://www.fireengineering.com/?p=210394 ; https://melsafetyinstitute.org/wp-content/uploads/2026/02/MSI-Fire-EMS-Bulletin-Apparatus-Equipment-Testing-for-Fire-Departments-Feb-2026.pdf
- **Aerial testing:** **annual** (visual, operational, load tests) plus a **5-year nondestructive test (NDT)** by an ASNT Level II technician. Do it sooner after overstress, fire impingement, or an accident. Legacy citation: NFPA 1914, then 1911, now 1910. Sources: https://education.vfis.com/Portals/0/Documents/Vehicle-Operations/VFIS-Aerial-Device-Maintenance-and-Inspection.pdf ; https://industrial-ia.com/annual-testing-and-five-year-ndt-for-aerial-devices/ ; https://www.ul.com/services/fire-department-aerial-device-testing-and-inspection
- **Apparatus retirement:** NFPA 1911 historically recommended moving apparatus older than 15 years to reserve and retiring at 25 years **[UNVERIFIED in this session; from general knowledge, appendix guidance, not a mandate; confirm in NFPA 1910 annex]**.
- **Ambulances / DOT / state:** Ambulance design is now NFPA 1900 (formerly 1917). The GSA KKK-A-1822 spec was set to sunset, and CAAS publishes its own Ground Vehicle Standard (GVS). Current GVS version was **not confirmed**. Older reporting says ~40 states use KKK in some form. **State ambulance licensing and inspection is state-specific**: SomaOS needs a per-state permit/inspection object with expiry. Sources: https://www.firerescue1.com/fire-products/vehicles/ambulances/articles/understanding-nfpas-new-ambulance-rule-kR1HSYxZ6KGbdpro/ ; https://www.jems.com/ems-operations/ems-equipment-gear/nfpa-draft-presents-potential/ . FMCSA/DOT commercial vehicle annual inspection applicability to fire apparatus (often exempt) was **not researched [UNVERIFIED]**.
- **Preventive maintenance:** PM schedules triggered by time, miles, engine hours, or pump hours, whichever comes first. Work orders carry parts, labor, and vendor (EVT technician). Cost per unit and per mile. [design recommendation]

## Data-model implications (summary for SomaOS architects)

1. **`Location` tree** (arbitrary depth, typed) with `dea_role` and `template_id`. Members and kits can also act as locations.
2. **`Item` (catalog SKU)** → GTIN(s), NDC/UDI-DI, category (medical, controlled schedule, station supply, PPE), unit of measure and pack conversions, `is_lot_tracked`, `is_serialized`, `is_controlled` + schedule.
3. **`Lot`** (item, lot number, expiry, recall flags) and **`StockBalance`** (location × item × lot) for FEFO and recalls.
4. **`InventoryTransaction` ledger** (immutable, double-entry from → to location). Types: receive, transfer, consume (with `epcr_encounter_id`), waste, destroy, adjust, count, restock-from-hospital. Signatures: `performed_by`, `witnessed_by`, method, timestamp, device. Controlled-substance rows add `standing_order_id` / `verbal_order_by`, `dea_registration_id`, plus notification deadlines (72h, one business day, 30-day designated-location wait).
5. **`DeaRegistration`** (state, number, expiry, schedules) → `RegisteredLocation`, `DesignatedLocation` (DEA notified date, active-from date).
6. **`Container`** (drug box / kit / bag) = location + serialized asset + `current_seal_no` + seal history.
7. **`Asset`** (serialized: apparatus, SCBA, cylinder, monitor, ladder, hose length, gear) with status (in service / OOS / reserve / retired), assignment (member, unit, location), meters (miles, engine hours, pump hours), manufacture date, in-service date, warranty, cost, depreciation.
8. **`ComplianceStandard` + `InspectionTemplate` + `Schedule`** (time-, usage-, or event-triggered, such as "after heat-label change" or "after aerial overstress") → **`Inspection/TestRecord`** (pass/fail, readings, attachments, tester certification such as ASNT Level II) → **`WorkOrder`** auto-created on fail → status change on the asset.
9. **`Check`** (daily/shift truck check) generated from load-out templates, offline-capable mobile, with photo, signature, and discrepancy capture.
10. **Cross-module events:** asset OOS → scheduling/staffing/CAD unit status; ePCR med/supply use → pending consumption → restock request; low stock → PO suggestion; expiry/recall → alert.
11. **Per-jurisdiction rule configuration** (state DEA/board of pharmacy, state ambulance licensing, adopted NFPA editions).
